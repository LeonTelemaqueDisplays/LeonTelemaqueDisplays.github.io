About

Consumer behaviour is not a discipline. It's a lens.

The data tells you where the system broke. The behaviour tells you why.

I didn't come to product and UX research through a design program. I came through consumer behaviour — and it turns out the frameworks for understanding why people buy things, why they stay, and why they leave are closer to product design than most designers think.

My academic background in Marketing and Consumer Culture gives me a different entry point into product systems. I study why consumers make decisions the same way a behavioural economist would — through the lens of psychology, incentive design, and system architecture. That lens applies directly to fintech products, subscription platforms, and media strategy.

On the research side I analyse consumer decision patterns, retention funnels, and behavioural signals across designed systems — building the case for why users act the way they do and what product decisions can shift those patterns.

On the design side I think in systems — mapping how individual product decisions ripple across user segments, time horizons, and business outcomes simultaneously. Every design decision serves some users and challenges others. The question is whether that trade-off is intentional.

The two sides are not separate disciplines. The research tells you where the design broke. The design tells you why the research looks the way it does.
