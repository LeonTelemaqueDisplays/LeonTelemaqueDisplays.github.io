About

Consumer behaviour is not a discipline. It's a lens.

The data tells you where the system broke. The behaviour tells you why.

I didn't come to product and UX research through a design program. I came through consumer behaviour — and it turns out the frameworks for understanding why people buy things, why they stay, and why they leave are closer to product design than most designers think.

My academic background in Marketing and Consumer Culture gives me a different entry point into product systems. I study why consumers make decisions the same way a behavioural economist would — through the lens of psychology, incentive design, and system architecture. That lens applies directly to brand and CPG strategy, fintech products, and subscription platforms.

On the research side I analyse consumer decision patterns, retention funnels, and behavioural signals across designed systems — building the case for why users act the way they do and what product decisions can shift those patterns.

On the design side I think in systems — mapping how individual product decisions ripple across user segments, time horizons, and business outcomes simultaneously. Every design decision serves some users and challenges others. The question is whether that trade-off is intentional.

The two sides are not separate disciplines. The research tells you where the design broke. The design tells you why the research looks the way it does.

How I Work

Process? Yeah, I have one.

Every case study on this site follows the same four moves — whether it's a two-day teardown or a six-week build.

01
Frame the Question
State the assumption or decision under scrutiny, and what's actually at stake if it's wrong.
02
Build the Model
SQL, Python, Streamlit — construct the analysis from real or rigorously modelled data, not a template.
03
Argue Both Sides
State the strongest case for the conclusion, then the strongest case against it. If it can't survive its own rebuttal, it isn't a conclusion yet.
04
Ship the Verdict
A clear recommendation, reasoned on statistical and practical significance — not just correlation.

Systems & Strategy

How brands and platforms build, defend, and decide

Decision architecture and brand architecture turn out to be the same problem: what to build, what to protect, and what to walk away from. Costco's flat margins, Tim Hortons' menu bloat, Wealthsimple's product sequencing, Roblox's platform failures, and Gatorade's category defense are the same question in five different industries — how a team decides what a brand or platform actually is, and defends that decision under pressure. This is the connective layer between the brand, fintech, and retention work above.

Platform Strategy · Costco
A Lifestyle Platform — Why Costco's "Broken" Model Works
2 to 3% retail margin. A $1.50 hot dog unchanged since 1985. A paid membership every competitor has tried and failed to copy. A teardown of why Costco isn't a retailer at all — it's a membership platform engineered around the psychology of winning, and why Walmart+ can subsidise the same hot dog and still can't buy the trust behind it.
Sources: Costco Earnings Calls
