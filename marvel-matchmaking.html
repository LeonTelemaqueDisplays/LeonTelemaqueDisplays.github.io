Marvel Rivals — Behavioural Matchmaking Fairness System
SYSTEMS DESIGN · MR-MATCH-001
01 · Why Matchmaking Fails
The Assumption
Most matchmaking systems treat fairness as a statistics problem. Equal MMR = fair match. Win probability balanced to 50/50. The math checks out.
The Reality
Players don't experience statistics. They experience agency, readability, and coordination. A statistically balanced match feels deeply unfair when one team has a 5-stack and the other has five solos.
The Actual Problem
Fairness in hero shooters fails across three dimensions: coordination asymmetry (solo vs stacks), composition volatility (chaotic team comps), and perception breakdown (players can't read why they lost).
The Design Constraint
Role Queue solves coordination and composition — but destroys player identity and agency. Marvel Rivals is built on freedom. The system must preserve it while controlling variance.

! CONSTRAINT
Permanent structural limitations: Some failure modes cannot be eliminated — only normalized. Party system communication inequality, snowball mechanics from game design decisions, and player behavioural volatility are inherent constraints. The system focuses on coordination normalization and perception preservation, not structural removal. Fairness is achieved through interpretability, not elimination of variance.
06 · Experimentation Design — Validating the System
→ WHY
A system design without an experimentation plan is a hypothesis. This section defines how the coordination density matching model would be validated in production — including primary metrics, guardrail metrics, and what failure looks like.
Experiment 01 — Core Fairness Hypothesis
Does coordination density matching improve perceived fairness?
A/B · 50/50 SPLIT
Control (A)
Standard MMR-only matchmaking. No coordination density scoring. Current production behaviour.
Treatment (B)
Coordination density matching active. Teams assembled within ±0.4 coordination density band before MMR balancing applied.
Unit of Randomisation
Player-level (not match-level) to avoid contamination. Player stays in assigned group for the full experiment window.
Metrics
PRIMARY (must move)
Post-match fairness rating (in-game prompt, 1–5)
Rage quit rate per player per week
D7 retention rate by cohort
GUARDRAILS (must not regress)
Average queue wait time (≤ +15% tolerance)
Match completion rate (no drop below 94%)
Win rate distribution variance (± 2% ceiling)
SAMPLE SIZE
~5,000 players / group
DURATION
14 days (2 full play-weeks)
POWER
80% · α = 0.05 · MDE: 5% rage quit reduction
NOVELTY BIAS CONTROL
First 3 days excluded from primary analysis
Experiment 02 — Composition Pressure Hypothesis
Does soft 2-2-2 role pressure reduce early-game composition failures without reducing hero diversity?
A/B/C · 3-WAY
Control (A) — No Pressure
Free hero selection, no role weighting applied. Baseline composition volatility.
Treatment B — Soft Pressure
Lobby UI shows team role distribution. Subtle prompt if composition is 4+ of one role. No restriction.
Treatment C — Matchmaking Weighting
Matchmaking deprioritises extreme comp pairings (e.g. 5 DPS vs 3 DPS/2 tank). No hard locks.
PRIMARY METRIC
Match completion rate · First-blood-to-surrender time
GUARDRAIL
Hero diversity index must not drop >5%
EXPECTED WINNER
B or C — hypothesis is either works vs. doing nothing
! FAILURE MODES
What a failed experiment looks like: If queue times increase >15% with no fairness rating improvement, the coordination band threshold (±0.4) is too tight — widen it. If rage quit rate drops but D7 retention doesn't move, the frustration signal is being suppressed not addressed — investigate match quality. If hero diversity index drops >5% in Experiment 02-C, the composition weighting is too aggressive and restricting identity — back off the multiplier.
07 · Core Design Insight
Defining Claim
"Fairness in competitive matchmaking is not statistical equilibrium — it is interpretable agency under constraint. A player who loses but can say 'I could have played that differently' has experienced a fair match. A player who can't explain why they lost has not, regardless of what the numbers say."
What The System Preserves
Hero freedom. Player identity. The ability to make meaningful individual decisions. No role locks, no hero bans, no forced queue type. The system controls variance — not expression.
What The System Controls
Coordination asymmetry between teams. Composition volatility extremes. Perceived unfairness from unreadable loss conditions. The gap between statistical balance and experienced balance.
Why This Matters For Retention
Players churn when they feel powerless, not when they lose. A system that preserves agency and readability reduces rage-quit events, sustains mid-lifecycle engagement, and extends the perceived fairness window — the same lifecycle mechanics that apply in any live-service product.
Residual Design Tension
The system cannot fully eliminate coordination asymmetry — party-based communication is structural to the game. Some volatility, some mismatch, and some snowball outcomes are permanent. The system manages perception of these events, not their existence.
