Design Philosophy
Active Principle
Progression as Identity Formation
The most powerful progression systems don't just make players stronger — they make players feel like a specific kind of person. When XP curves, skill unlocks, and build choices align, players stop saying "I leveled up" and start saying "I'm a berserker" or "I'm a tactician." That identity investment is what retains them.
Diablo IV WoW Monster Hunter
Real World Reference
Diablo Series Paragon system solved post-cap retention by making progression infinite without invalidating mastery.
Monster Hunter Unlock speed deliberately slow — each weapon type mastery is a separate identity investment.
Path of Exile Maximum build diversity, extreme respec cost. Rewards commitment, punishes indecision.
Pokémon Low difficulty ramp creates accessibility but sacrifices mastery satisfaction for veteran players.
Warframe High quest density risks purpose dilution — players often lose track of what they're working toward.
Behavioral Principle
Delayed Competence & Mastery Investment
Delayed competence increases long-term investment only if players receive meaningful feedback before frustration dominates. The window between confusion and capability is where most players quit — or commit for life.
